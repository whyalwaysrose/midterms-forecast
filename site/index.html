How this works

The model

Every race is tracked on the logit of the two-party Democratic share. A single national environment evolves as a Gaussian random walk driven by generic-ballot polls. Each race is that environment scaled by its own elasticity, plus a structural baseline and its own drift:

θr,t = αr + λr·ηt + εr,t

α comes from a fundamentals prior (presidential lean in 2024 and 2020, plus incumbency), λ is partially pooled across races, and ε is a race-specific random walk.

The polls

Polls observe that latent state through a biased instrument. The model estimates a house effect for each frequent pollster (constrained to sum to zero, or it would not be identified), an adjustment for likely-voter versus registered-voter versus adult samples, and a partisan-sponsor correction. A Student-t likelihood keeps one wild poll from dragging the estimate.

Why the intervals are wide

Polling misses are correlated. If all 35 races erred independently the errors would cancel and the seat forecast would be absurdly confident. So on top of the posterior we add a national error common to every race and a state-level error whose correlation decays with political distance between states. That correlation is what gives the seat distribution its real width.

Honest limits

This model predicts polls well; whether it predicts the election well depends on whether the polls are biased, which no amount of modelling can determine in advance. Races with no polling are carried entirely by fundamentals and should be read as rough. See the methodology document for the full specification and known caveats.
